How can I design the backend logic and database Entities to track user XP, handle level-ups, and award different tiers of trophies (bronze, silver, gold) based on 'requirementType' when a user completes a workout via a '/complete' endpoint?"

**How We Verified the Code:**

We also tested the grouping logic in `WorkoutController.java` to ensure trophies were correctly categorized by tier on the `/trophy` page.

## 2. Core Component: Smart Workout Generation Algorithm

**Feature Description:** 
An algorithm that automatically generates a customized daily workout plan based on a selected muscle group. Instead of purely random selection, it groups exercises by category and selects them based on specific ratios (e.g., targeting upper, middle, and lower chest proportionally).

**Primary Prompts Used:**
> "Write a Java method for a Spring Boot controller that takes a muscle group string (e.g., 'Chest'), fetches a list of exercises from the database, groups them by their 'category', and randomly selects a specific number of exercises based on a predefined ratio array (e.g., 2:1:1). Return the final selected list to a Thymeleaf model."

**How We Verified the Code:**
* **Logic Testing:** We reviewed the generated `getMuscleConfig` and `getCategoryRatios` helper methods and manually adjusted the hardcoded arrays to fit our actual fitness design requirements.
* **UI Testing:** We ran the application, clicked on different templates (e.g., Chest vs. Abs), and observed the output on the `generate.html` page to ensure the algorithm strictly followed the designated quantity and category ratios.

## 3. Core Component: State Management & Data Consistency (Bug Fix)

**Feature Description:** 
Ensuring that the specific exercises randomly generated on the preview page are exactly the ones saved to the database when the user confirms the plan, preventing "state loss" or secondary re-randomization.

**Primary Prompts Used:**
> "In my actual run, the training exercises generated by the templates on the preview page are inconsistent with the exercises displayed on the final active workouts page. Currently, I am only passing the 'templateId' via the form. How do I pass the dynamic list of specific generated exercises from Thymeleaf back to the Java Controller's POST method to fix this state loss?"

**How We Verified the Code:**
* **Implementation:** Following the AI's suggestion, we added a loop of `<input type="hidden" name="exerciseIds" th:value="${ex.id}" />` in our `generate.html` form. We also updated `WorkoutController.java` to accept `@RequestParam List<Long> exerciseIds` and routed it to `generatePlanFromExercises`.
* **System Testing:** We performed an end-to-end test. We generated a "Shoulders" template, noted the exact 6 exercises on the preview screen, clicked "Start Workout", and verified that the active workout page displayed those exact same 6 exercises without re-rolling.

## Ethical Considerations & Bias Reflection (AI-Generated Code)

As part of our commitment to **Human-Centric Computing** (supporting equality, diversity, and inclusion), we critically reviewed the AI-generated logic and identified the following ethical considerations:

1. **Physical Capability Bias:** The AI generated default configurations for the workout plans (e.g., automatically assigning 4 sets and 12 reps for every exercise, or picking standard exercises from the database). This assumes the user is an able-bodied, experienced young adult. This creates a bias against the elderly, beginners, or individuals with physical limitations.
2. **Mitigation & Future Work:** To achieve true inclusive design, we realize that hardcoding these values is insufficient. In our design alternatives (documented in our Process Portfolio), we propose implementing a "Difficulty Level" or "Accessibility Mode" (e.g., Low-Impact, Seated Exercises) that dynamically alters the `getMuscleConfig` algorithm based on user capabilities, ensuring the gamified experience is accessible to everyone, not just traditional gym-goers.
